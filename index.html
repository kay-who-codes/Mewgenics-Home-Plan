    🧬 Breeding Basics

    • Traits stack. Kittens inherit a mix of both parents' traits — good and bad — so pairing two strong cats doesn't guarantee a strong litter.
    • Watch for recessive traits. Some negative traits can hide for a generation and pop back up later, so keep notes on a bloodline's history, not just its current stats.
    • Stat caps matter. Cats have soft caps on stats like Speed, Smarts, and Cute — breeding two maxed-out cats in the same stat has diminishing returns.
    • Retire your elites carefully. Your best cat is more valuable as a breeding parent than as an active runner once you have a good bloodline going.

    🏠 Room Guide

    • Elite Breeding (roof): Reserve this for your top-tier cats — the ones with the best trait combos you want to lock into your bloodline. Every pairing here should be deliberate.
    • Decent Cats (left room): A holding area for solid but non-elite cats. Good for cats you're still evaluating, or ones waiting for a specific partner to become available.
    • Eugenics (right room): For pairing off less-refined or newly acquired cats to test trait combinations and grow your roster before promoting the best results upstairs.

    💡 Tips

    • Track litters in a spreadsheet or notebook if you're aiming for a specific trait combo — genetics can take several generations to stabilize.
    • Don't cull too early — a cat with a mediocre stat line can still carry a valuable recessive trait for future litters.
    • Rotate cats between rooms as your bloodline improves, so "elite" always reflects your current best, not last week's best.
